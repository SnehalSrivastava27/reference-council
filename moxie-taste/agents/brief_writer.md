# Brief Writer

Produces the creator-facing collab brief. Stage 5, the deliverable.

## What this document is

A creator opens it, sees their own reel next to a Moxie reel, understands why
they were picked, and knows exactly what to shoot. It should be readable in
three minutes.

It is not a scorecard, not a contract, and not a style guide. Anything that
doesn't help them shoot the film comes out.

## Structure

Use `references/brief_template.md`. The order matters — praise before ask.
A creator who reads the ask first assumes they were picked at random.

1. **Why you** — two sentences. Personal, specific, names a reel.
2. **Your reels we loved** — the 2–3 shortlisted, with praise, each paired with
   its Moxie reference reel side by side.
3. **The film we want** — pillar, length, the one-line concept.
4. **The five things that matter** — background, lighting, pacing, hook, outfit,
   each as a specification tailored to her.
5. **Products and what to say** — SKUs, technique dialect, the framework.
6. **Deliverables and dates.**

## Writing the five specifications

This is where the shortlist pays off. Every specification should reference her
own work where possible.

**Background.** Name two specific locations, one brighter for the reveal. If she
has a location that scored well, name it: *"the room with the doorway from your
Diwali reel."* Never write "a clean, well-lit space" — that is not a
specification, it's a wish.

**Lighting.** Say where the hair light comes from and at what time of day. This
is the specification creators most often get wrong and the one that most changes
the footage. Be concrete: *"shoot the reveal between 4 and 6pm with the window
behind your left shoulder."*

**Pacing.** Give a number, not an adjective. *"Aim for roughly one cut per second
— about where your [reel name] already sits."* Add the shape: fast through the
application, slow down for the last eight seconds.

**Hook.** Write it, or write two options, in her cadence using the pattern she
already reaches for (see `references/hook_library.md`). Reference the reel where
she already did it. If her hook fit was "needs rebuilding", supply the hook
verbatim and say why — do not assume she'll improvise into the right register.

**Outfit.** Name colours and necklines, not a mood. Specify the change between
routine and reveal. List the two or three things to avoid — for her, against her
chosen backgrounds. *"Avoid the black hoodie from your GRWM reels — against that
wall your hair disappears into it."* — but phrase forward, not as criticism of
the old reel.

## Tone

Peer-to-peer, warm, specific. She should feel chosen, not processed.

Hinglish where she works in Hinglish. Keep the technical bits technical — the
creators in the reference set are competent and being talked down to reads worse
than being over-explained to.

## The thing that makes this work

The side-by-side. Her reel, then the Moxie reel, then one sentence naming the
overlap:

> *"Your reel and this one are doing the same thing — both open mid-sentence with
> a hand already moving, and both let the payoff run long instead of chopping it.
> That's the overlap we want in the collab."*

She is not being taught a house style. She is being shown that her instinct and
Moxie's are the same instinct. That is why briefs built this way come back
on-brand without micromanagement.

## Before sending — check

- [ ] No scores anywhere in the document
- [ ] No mention of her non-shortlisted reels
- [ ] Every specification references her own work where it can
- [ ] The hook is written, not described
- [ ] Two locations named, one brighter for the reveal
- [ ] The hair light source is stated explicitly
- [ ] Pacing given as a number
- [ ] Maximum three Moxie reference reels
- [ ] Nothing in it that doesn't help her shoot

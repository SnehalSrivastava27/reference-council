# Shortlist and Praise

Picks the 2–3 reels the creator will see, and writes what was good about each.
Stage 3. This is the stage that determines whether the creator engages.

## Selecting for spread, not score

Three high scorers demonstrating the same strength are worse than three reels
that between them show good background, good hook and good pacing.

The shortlist has a job: **point at three different things she already does that
Moxie wants.** If all three praise her lighting, she learns one thing. If they
praise her lighting, her hook and her cutting, she understands the standard.

Select so that:
- Each reel is the best example of a *different* parameter
- At least one is her single best instinct from the analysis
- Together they cover at least three of the five parameters at 4+

If the twelve genuinely only demonstrate one strength, shortlist two rather than
padding to three. A weak third reel dilutes the other two.

## Writing the praise

This is creator-facing. It is the part they actually read.

**Be specific to the point of being technical, then translate.** Vague praise
reads as form-letter and gets skimmed:

> Weak: "Loved the vibe on this one, great energy!"
> Strong: "The window behind you at 0:12 is putting light straight through your
> hair — that's the shot that made us reach out. It's exactly why your curls read
> as individual clumps instead of one dark mass, and it's the hardest thing to get
> right."

The second version tells her *what to repeat*. The first tells her nothing.

**Name the timestamp.** It proves you watched.

**Say why it matters, not just that it's good.** "Your cuts sit around one a
second" is an observation. "Your cuts sit around one a second, which is exactly
where our best-performing films land — it's why your demos never drag" is a
reason.

**One line per parameter, maximum three parameters per reel.** Praising all five
on one reel reads as flattery.

**Match the register.** Warm, specific, peer-to-peer. Hinglish where the creator
works in Hinglish. Never brand-voice, never "we were impressed by your
content!" — that is the tone of a form email and it gets treated like one.

## What never appears

- Scores or numbers out of 5
- Any mention of her other nine reels
- Any criticism, however gently framed
- Comparison to other creators
- The words "content", "creator economy", "deliverables" in the praise section

Criticism belongs in the internal scorecard. If a gap needs addressing, the brief
handles it as a *specification* ("shoot this one between 4 and 6pm near the
window"), never as feedback on past work.

## Output

```markdown
## Reel 1 — [her caption or a descriptor] — [link or filename]

**What we loved:**
- [Parameter]: specific observation with timestamp, and why it matters
- [Parameter]: same
- [Parameter]: same

**Closest Moxie reel:** [title] — [link]
**What they share:** one sentence naming the overlap

## Reel 2 — ...
```

Then hand to `brief_writer.md`.
